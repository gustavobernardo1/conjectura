## Statement as read from the primary source

I downloaded the TeX source of arXiv:1507.02341v3 (`https://arxiv.org/e-print/1507.02341v3`;
gzip, file date 2017-04-21) and read `DistanceCharPolyLogConcaveS.tex` in full
before I read the notes. I take the statement numbers from the theorem counters
in the TeX (shared counter, numbered within sections). I did not compile it.

- **Definitions (§1).** `D(G)` is the distance matrix, and `p_D(x) = det(xI − D(G))`.
  `δ_k(G)` is the coefficient of `x^k` in `det(D(G) − xI) = (−1)^n p_D(x)`. The
  order is assumed to be at least 3. For `0 ≤ k ≤ n−2`,
  `d_k(G) = 2^{−(n−2)} 2^k |δ_k(G)|`. *Unimodal*: there is a `k` with
  `a_{i−1} ≤ a_i` for `i ≤ k` and `a_i ≥ a_{i+1}` for `i ≥ k`. *Log-concave*:
  `a_j² ≥ a_{j−1}a_{j+1}`. "Peak" is not defined separately, so ties are not
  settled by the source.
- **Fact 1.1** (Graham–Lovász, eq. (44)): for a tree `T` on `n` vertices,
  `(−1)^{n−1} δ_k(T) > 0` for `0 ≤ k ≤ n−2`.
- **Conjecture 1.2** (Collins–Shor): "The [normalized] coefficients of the
  distance characteristic polynomial for any tree `T` with `n` vertices are
  unimodal with peak between `⌊n/2⌋` and `⌈n − n/√5⌉`." Quantifier: every tree
  of order `n ≥ 3`. In the strong reading the project uses, every index at which
  `d_0, …, d_{n−2}` attains its maximum lies in `[⌊n/2⌋, ⌈n − n/√5⌉]`.
- **Theorem 2.1** (iii): for a tree, `d_0(T), …, d_{n−2}(T)` is log-concave and
  unimodal. I read its proof (real-rootedness, then Lemma 1.4 and Fact 1.1).
- **Observation 3.1:** `ℓ_T(x) = −2^{−(n−2)} det(2xI − D(T))` is real-rooted,
  with coefficient `−4` for `x^n`, `0` for `x^{n−1}`, and `d_k(T) > 0` for `x^k`,
  `0 ≤ k ≤ n−2`. I checked the coefficient part myself:
  `det(2xI − D) = (−1)^n Σ δ_k (2x)^k`, so the coefficient of `x^k` in `ℓ_T` is
  `(−1)^{n−1} δ_k 2^{k−(n−2)}`, which equals `d_k` by Fact 1.1.

**The claim, as I read it before the proof.** For integers `a, c ≥ 1` with
`a + c ≥ 3`, `K(a,0,c)` is the tree with path `v_1 v_2 v_3`, `a` pendant leaves
at `v_1`, `c` at `v_3` and none at `v_2`. Its order is `n = a + c + 3 ≥ 6`. Put
`m = ⌊n/2⌋`. T3 claims `d_{m−1} < d_m` and `d_{m+1} < d_m`, hence that `m` is
the unique maximizing index and that Conjecture 1.2 holds for `K(a,0,c)` in the
strong reading.

The Corollary is conditional on T2. It claims that every caterpillar of
diameter 4 has `⌊n/2⌋` as its unique maximizing index.

My own description of the family, from the definition of a caterpillar (deleting
all leaves leaves a path), before reading the notes:
- a tree of diameter 4 has a longest path `v_0 v_1 v_2 v_3 v_4`;
- if it is a caterpillar, deleting the leaves leaves a path of diameter 2, that is `v_1 v_2 v_3`;
- `v_1` and `v_3` are not leaves of `T` but are ends of that path, so each carries `≥ 1` leaf, and `v_2` carries `b ≥ 0` leaves;
- conversely, every such tree is a caterpillar of diameter 4.

So the family is exactly the `K(a,b,c)` with `a, c ≥ 1`, `b ≥ 0`, and `n ≥ 5`.

What the notes attribute to the source matches the source:
- the definitions of `δ_k`, `d_k` and `ℓ_T`;
- Fact 1.1 (sign of `δ_k`);
- Observation 3.1 (`d_k` are the coefficients of `ℓ_T`);
- Theorem 2.1 (log-concavity);
- Conjecture 1.2.
