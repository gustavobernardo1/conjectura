## Statement as read from the primary source

Source: G. Aalipour, A. Abiad, Z. Berikkyzy, L. Hogben, F. H. J. Kenter,
J. C.-H. Lin, M. Tait, *Proof of a conjecture of Graham and Lovász concerning
unimodality of coefficients of the distance characteristic polynomial of a
tree*, arXiv:1507.02341v3 (20 April 2017). I read the full PDF (11 pages)
before reading the claim.

- **Definitions (§1).** `D(G)` is the distance matrix,
  `p_D(x) = det(xI − D(G))`, and `δ_k(G)` is the coefficient of `x^k` in
  `det(D(G) − xI) = (−1)^n p_D(x)`. The order is assumed to be at least 3. For
  `0 ≤ k ≤ n−2`, `d_k(G) = (1/2^{n−2}) 2^k |δ_k(G)|` are the normalized
  coefficients. Unimodal means there is a `k` with `a_{i−1} ≤ a_i` for `i ≤ k`
  and `a_i ≥ a_{i+1}` for `i ≥ k`. "Peak" is not defined separately, so the
  treatment of ties is left open.
- **Fact 1.1** ([Graham–Lovász, eq. (44)]). For a tree `T` on `n` vertices,
  `(−1)^{n−1} δ_k(T) > 0` for `0 ≤ k ≤ n−2`. Hence
  `d_k(T) = (−1)^{n−1} δ_k(T)/2^{n−k−2}`.
- **Conjecture 1.2 (Collins–Shor).** "The [normalized] coefficients of the
  distance characteristic polynomial for any tree `T` with `n` vertices are
  unimodal with peak between `⌊n/2⌋` and `⌈n − n/√5⌉`."
  - Quantifiers: for every tree `T` of order `n ≥ 3`.
  - Conclusion: `d_0(T), …, d_{n−2}(T)` is unimodal, and its peak lies in
    `[⌊n/2⌋, ⌈n − n/√5⌉]`.
- **Theorem 2.1.** For a tree `T` of order `n`: (i) the coefficient sequence of
  `p_D` is log-concave; (ii) `|δ_0|, …, |δ_{n−2}|` is log-concave and unimodal;
  (iii) `d_0(T), …, d_{n−2}(T)` is log-concave and unimodal.
- **Observation 3.1.** `ℓ_T(x) = −(1/2^{n−2}) det(2xI − D(T))` is real-rooted,
  with coefficient `−4` for `x^n`, `0` for `x^{n−1}`, and `d_k(T) > 0` for
  `x^k`, `0 ≤ k ≤ n−2`.

**Claim (spine3.md, T2).** For integers `a, b, c ≥ 1`, let `K(a,b,c)` be the
tree with spine path `v_1 v_2 v_3` in which `v_1, v_2, v_3` carry `a, b, c`
pendant leaves. Put `n = a+b+c+3 ≥ 6` and `m = ⌊n/2⌋`. Then (1)
`d_{m−1} < d_m` and (2) `d_{m+1} < d_m`. Consequently `m` is the unique
maximizing index, and Conjecture 1.2 holds for `K(a,b,c)` in either reading of
ties. The claim's notation (`δ_k`, `d_k`, `ℓ_T`) agrees with the source. What
the notes attribute to Fact 1.1, Observation 3.1, Theorem 2.1 and
Conjecture 1.2 matches what the source states.
